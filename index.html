What you get

Everything a training log actually needs

Built around the four things you check mid-set: what you lifted last time, how much rest is left, whether this is a personal best, and where the session is going.

Fast set logging

Weight and reps per set, ticked off as you go. Previous performance sits right next to the field so you never have to remember what you lifted last week. Estimated one-rep max updates as you type.

Rest timer that behaves

Starts automatically the moment you complete a set, or on demand. Add 15 seconds without breaking flow, and it survives a page reload mid-countdown.

Personal records, tracked honestly

Three separate records per exercise — heaviest weight, most reps, and highest estimated one-rep max. When you beat one, the session summary tells you exactly which.

Progress you can read

Weekly volume bars, muscle-group balance across completed sets, lifetime totals and a day streak. Charts are drawn as inline SVG — no charting library, no third-party request.

Exercise library, extensible

Around ninety common movements grouped by muscle and equipment, searchable as you type. Anything missing — a landmine variation, a machine your gym owns — you add once and it stays.

Export, import, walk away

Your whole history is one JSON document. Export a backup any time, import it into another browser, or erase everything from the settings screen. No vendor lock, because there is no vendor.

Workflow

Three taps from the rack to the record

  1. 1

    Start a session

    Begin empty, repeat your last workout with its sets and weights already filled in, or pick a Push / Pull / Legs split and get a solid compound for each group.

  2. 2

    Log as you train

    Add exercises from the searchable library, enter weight and reps, tick the set. The rest timer starts itself and the clock keeps running in the header.

  3. 3

    Finish and keep the evidence

    The summary reports volume, sets, duration and any personal records you just broke. Everything is written to local storage immediately, so a closed tab never costs you a session.

Privacy by architecture

There is no server to leak your data from

IronLog is a static site. It has no backend, no database, no analytics script and no account system, so there is nothing to breach and nothing to sell.

Your workouts live in your browser's local storage under a single versioned key. They are never transmitted — the only network request this page makes is for its own HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Clear your browser's site data and the log goes with it, which is exactly why the export button exists.

  • No sign-upNothing to register, verify or remember.
  • No trackingNo analytics, pixels, cookies or third-party fonts.
  • No dependencies at runtimeVanilla ES modules and inline SVG. No CDN calls.
  • Portable by defaultOne JSON export holds your entire training history.

Questions

Things worth knowing

Is it really free?

Yes. It is a static site published under the MIT licence. There is no paid tier and no plan to add one, because there is no running cost — hosting is GitHub Pages and the app is plain files.

Will my data survive if I close the browser?

Yes, as long as the browser keeps its local storage for this site. Every change is written immediately rather than on save. Clearing site data or using a private window will lose it, so export a backup occasionally.

Can I use it on my phone?

The layout is mobile-first with a bottom tab bar and thumb-sized set rows. It ships a web app manifest, so on Android you can add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen. On iOS, Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen does the same.

Does it work without a connection?

Once the page has loaded, yes — all logic runs locally and no data is fetched. A reload while offline depends on your browser's HTTP cache, since there is no service worker caching the shell.

How is the one-rep max calculated?

With the Epley formula, weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). It is an estimate that drifts at high rep ranges, so treat it as a progress signal rather than a tested maximum.

Where is the source?

In the repository this page is deployed from. The app lives under /app as plain ES modules with no build step, and the training maths is unit-tested in Node.
