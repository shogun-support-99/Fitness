IronLog is a strength training journal that runs entirely in your browser. Weight, reps, rest and the record you broke — written down as you train, and never sent anywhere. No sign-up wall, no subscription, no analytics.

What the log does

Built around the four things you check mid-set: what you lifted last time, how much rest is left, whether this is a personal best, and where the session is going.

Set logging
Weight and reps per set, ticked off as you go. Last session's figure sits inside the field after your number, so you never have to remember what you lifted last week. Estimated one-rep max updates as you type.
Rest timer
Starts the moment you tick a set done, or on demand. Add fifteen seconds without breaking flow, and it survives a page reload mid-countdown.
Personal records
Three separate records per exercise — heaviest weight, most reps, and highest estimated one-rep max. When you beat one, the session summary names exactly which.
Progress
Weekly volume, muscle-group balance across completed sets, lifetime totals and a training-day streak. Charts are drawn as inline SVG — no charting library, no third-party request.
Templates
Repeat your last session with its exercises, sets and weights prefilled, or start from a push / pull / legs split and make it your own.
Your data, portable
Export the whole history as one JSON document, import it back, or erase it. Every change is written immediately rather than on save.

1,388 exercises, and how to do them

Around ninety curated staples plus an open catalogue of 1,316 movements, searchable as you type and ranked by relevance. Every catalogue entry carries numbered step-by-step instructions in ten languages, so the sheet can tell you how the lift goes without leaving the rack.

The catalogue is fetched after the app paints, and each language pack loads only when you first open an exercise in it — so the app stays quick and a monolingual reader never downloads nine packs they will not read.

There is no server to leak your data from

IronLog is a static site. It has no backend, no database, no analytics script and no account system, so there is nothing to breach and nothing to sell.

Your workouts live in your browser's local storage under a single versioned key. They are never transmitted. The only requests this page makes are for its own HTML, CSS, JavaScript and two font files it hosts itself. Clear your browser's site data and the log goes with it, which is exactly why the export button exists.

The trade-off is worth stating plainly rather than hiding: because storage is per-browser and per-origin, your log does not sync between devices. Export a backup if your history matters to you.

Things worth knowing

Is it really free?

Yes. It is a static site published under the MIT licence. There is no paid tier and no plan to add one, because there is no running cost — hosting is GitHub Pages and the app is plain files.

Will my data survive if I close the browser?

Yes, as long as the browser keeps its local storage for this site. Every change is written immediately rather than on save. Clearing site data or using a private window will lose it, so export a backup occasionally.

Can I use it on my phone?

The layout is mobile-first with a bottom tab bar and thumb-sized set rows. It ships a web app manifest, so on Android you can add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen. On iOS, Safari's Share then Add to Home Screen does the same.

Does it work without a connection?

Once the page has loaded, yes — all logic runs locally and no data is fetched. A reload while offline depends on your browser's HTTP cache, since there is no service worker caching the shell.

How is the one-rep max calculated?

With the Epley formula, weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). It is an estimate that drifts at high rep ranges, so treat it as a progress signal rather than a tested maximum.

Where do the exercises and instructions come from?

The catalogue data and instruction text come from the MIT-licensed hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset, attributed in the app and in the repository. That project's exercise media is separately licensed to Gym Visual and is not redistributed here, so no exercise shows a GIF or video.

Where is the source?

In the repository this page is deployed from. The app lives under /app as plain ES modules with no build step, and the training maths is unit-tested in Node.
